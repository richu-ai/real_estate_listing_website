Land Buying Guide

Everything you need to know before purchasing land - from finding the perfect property to closing the deal

Introduction to Land Buying

Purchasing land is different from buying an existing home. Without a structure to evaluate, you'll need to focus on the land itself, its potential uses, and any restrictions that may apply. This guide will walk you through the entire process, helping you make informed decisions and avoid common pitfalls.

1

Determine Your Needs

Identify your purpose for buying land - building a home, investment, agriculture, or recreation. This will guide your search criteria.

2

Set Your Budget

Consider not just the purchase price but also taxes, development costs, utilities, and maintenance expenses.

3

Research Locations

Look beyond the property itself to the surrounding area - zoning laws, future development plans, and community amenities.

The Buying Process

Purchasing land involves several key steps that differ from traditional home buying. Here's what to expect:

  • Property Search: Work with a land specialist who understands the unique aspects of vacant land transactions.
  • Due Diligence: Conduct thorough research on zoning, restrictions, access, utilities, and environmental factors.
  • Financing: Land loans typically require larger down payments (20-50%) and have higher interest rates than traditional mortgages.
  • Survey & Title: Always get a current survey and title insurance to verify boundaries and ownership.
  • Closing: Be prepared for additional documentation and potential delays compared to home purchases.

Pre-Purchase Checklist

Verify zoning and permitted uses with local authorities
Check for easements, rights-of-way, or other encumbrances
Confirm access to utilities or costs to bring them to the property
Research soil conditions and perc tests for septic systems
Investigate potential environmental concerns or protected areas

Key Considerations

Zoning and Land Use

Zoning laws dictate what you can and cannot do with your property. Always verify:

  • Current zoning classification
  • Allowed uses (residential, commercial, agricultural, etc.)
  • Minimum lot size requirements
  • Setback requirements
  • Future zoning changes in the area

Access and Utilities

Don't assume the property has legal access or utilities available:

  • Verify legal access via a deeded easement or public road
  • Check availability and costs for water, sewer, electricity, gas, and internet
  • For rural properties, investigate well and septic system requirements

Important Warning

Never rely solely on a seller's verbal assurances about zoning, utilities, or building potential. Always verify this information independently with the appropriate government agencies before purchasing.

Financing Your Land Purchase

Financing land purchases can be more challenging than financing homes. Here are your main options:

  • Land Loans: Short-term loans (2-5 years) specifically for vacant land, often requiring 20-50% down.
  • Construction Loans: Combine land purchase and building costs into one loan, converting to a mortgage after completion.
  • Seller Financing: Some landowners offer direct financing, often with more flexible terms.
  • Home Equity: Use equity from an existing property to finance the land purchase.
  • Cash Purchase: The simplest option if you have the funds available.
