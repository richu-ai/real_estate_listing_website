Zoning Variance & Special Use Permits

If your intended use doesn't comply with current zoning, you may need to apply for a variance or special use permit. The process typically involves:

  • Application Submission: Complete forms with detailed project description
  • Public Notice: Notification to adjacent property owners
  • Public Hearing: Presentation before zoning board
  • Board Decision: Approval, denial, or approval with conditions
  • Appeal Period: Typically 30 days to appeal decision

Zoning FAQ

How do I find out the zoning of a property?

Zoning information is available through your local planning or zoning department. Many municipalities provide online zoning maps. You can also check the property's deed or contact the county assessor's office.

Can zoning laws change after I purchase property?

Yes, zoning laws can change through a process called rezoning. However, existing structures typically enjoy "grandfather" protection, meaning they can continue their current use even if new zoning would prohibit it.

What's the difference between zoning and building codes?

Zoning regulates land use (what you can build), while building codes regulate construction standards (how you build it). Both must be complied with for any development project.

How long does a zoning variance take to obtain?

The variance process typically takes 2-4 months, depending on the municipality. Complex cases or appeals can take longer. Special use permits may require additional time for environmental reviews.

What are setback requirements?

Setbacks specify how far structures must be from property lines, roads, or other features. They vary by zoning district and may have different requirements for front, side, and rear yards.
